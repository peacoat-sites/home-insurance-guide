Roughly 44% of Airbnb hosts in the United States have no idea their standard homeowner's policy won't cover a guest injury. I know this because I spent 14 years on the other side of that phone call, listening to hosts describe what happened and then waiting while they processed the silence I had to give them.

The problem isn't that hosts are careless. It's that the insurance industry has never been great at advertising its own exclusions. Your standard HO-3 policy was written for owner-occupied dwellings. The moment you hand keys to a paying stranger, the legal and actuarial math changes. Insurers know this. They're just not obligated to remind you at renewal.

## What Your Homeowner's Policy Actually Does (and Doesn't) Cover

Most HO-3 policies contain a "business pursuits" exclusion. If you're collecting rental income, many insurers treat that as a business activity, which voids coverage for losses tied to that activity. The exclusion isn't buried in fine print; it's usually in Section I, under "Perils Insured Against" or the exclusions rider. I've watched adjusters flip to it in under 30 seconds when a claim came in from an Airbnb address.

What this means in practice: if a guest accidentally starts a kitchen fire, your insurer may deny the structure claim on the grounds that the home was operating as a rental at the time. Your personal [liability coverage](/home-insurance-liability-coverage/), which typically runs $100,000 to $300,000 on a standard policy, almost certainly won't respond to a guest's slip-and-fall lawsuit either.

Some insurers have softened their positions on occasional rentals, particularly for hosts renting fewer than 30 days per year. A few carriers offer an STR endorsement that bolts onto your existing HO-3 for an additional $200 to $600 annually. But "occasional" is doing real work in that sentence. If you're renting 90 nights a year, no reasonable person should rely on a policy designed for someone who Airbnbs their beach house twice in August.

One thing that catches hosts off guard: your [personal property coverage](/homeowners-insurance-personal-property-coverage/) almost always has a sublimit, sometimes as low as $500, for property stolen from a residence while it's rented to others. Laptop, camera, a watch left in a drawer. Gone from your perspective long before an adjuster even opens a file.

## AirCover: What Airbnb Is Selling You and What It Actually Is

Airbnb rebranded its host protections under the "AirCover" label in 2022, and the marketing works. "$3 million in damage protection" sounds like serious coverage. And honestly, compared to where things stood five years ago, it is an improvement. But it is not insurance. It's a host guarantee program, which means Airbnb controls the claims process, Airbnb decides what qualifies, and Airbnb can change the terms without regulatory oversight.

AirCover covers physical damage to your home and belongings caused by guests. It does NOT cover:

- Injuries to guests on your property (no liability protection)
- Damage from pets brought by guests, unless you specifically allowed pets
- Loss of cash, securities, or collectibles
- Damage to shared or common areas (if you're in a condo, the HOA likely matters more here)
- Consequential losses like lost future bookings while repairs happen

That liability gap is the one that should concern you most. A guest falls down your stairs, breaks a wrist, and hires an attorney. AirCover won't touch it. According to the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC), liability claims from rental properties have been rising year over year as short-term rental volume grows. A single premises liability lawsuit can run well past $500,000 in legal fees and settlement before a verdict is even reached.

I had a host tell me she thought AirCover was "basically like insurance." It's closer to a warranty with Airbnb as the judge. File the claim through proper channels anyway, because it does [pay out](/nearly-half-of-home-insurance-claims-now-pay-out-nothing/) for guest damage, but don't let it replace a real policy.

## Three Scenarios That Show Why This Matters

**Host rents a spare bedroom 15 nights per year, has standard HO-3, files a guest-damage claim** → Insurer investigates, determines rental activity, invokes business pursuits exclusion → Claim denied; host pays $6,400 in water damage repairs out of pocket.

**Host in Nashville rents primary home for 80 nights, buys dedicated STR policy for $1,950/year, guest slips on wet porch** → Guest files premises liability claim for $85,000 → STR insurer defends and settles; host pays $0 out of pocket beyond the deductible.

**Host relies on AirCover, guest damages $2,200 worth of furniture** → Files AirCover claim with photos and receipts → AirCover pays $1,850 after depreciation and exclusions → Host covers the gap. Not a disaster, but not whole either.

## The Conversation You Need to Have Before Your First Booking

Call your homeowner's insurer. Not email. Call. Ask specifically: "Does my policy exclude or limit coverage when the property is used as a short-term rental for compensation?" Write down the representative's name and the date. Then ask if they offer an STR endorsement, and what the restrictions are.

If they say you're covered without any endorsement and no questions about rental frequency, I'd be skeptical enough to get that in writing. Insurers sometimes give inaccurate verbal guidance, and at claims time, what matters is the policy language, not what a service rep told you in May.

The Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS) also recommends upgrading physical security before any rental period: deadbolts on interior doors with valuables, smart locks with rotating codes between guests, and working smoke and CO detectors on every floor. Some STR insurers will actually lower your premium if you document these installations, which is a detail I've seen hosts miss because they never thought to ask.

A [water leak sensor](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=water+leak+sensor+home&tag=contentportfo-20) placed under sinks and near water heaters is worth every dollar when you have a stranger in your home for a week. Guests aren't always careful. (The site may earn a commission on that link.)

As of August 2026, the short-term rental insurance market has matured enough that you have real options. Dedicated policies exist. Endorsements are available from major carriers. There's no excuse left for running a rental business under a personal lines policy and hoping nothing happens.
